Hull Inspection AI

An underwater inspection drone (ROV) photographs a ship's hull. Two machine-learning models look for corrosion in each photo, and every result is checked against masks drawn by people. This handbook covers the data, the models, how they were evaluated, the simulation, the dashboard, and how to run everything.

01Summary

The U-Net finds corrosion in held-out test photos with a mean Dice score of 0.655 (pixel overlap with the human mask). On the 144 corroded test tiles it missed 3; the SVM baseline missed 43. Train, validation and test segmentation scores agree within 0.03, so the U-Net is not overfitted. Both models score lower on test tiles than on validation tiles, mainly because the test split contains only 21 clearly clean tiles; the SVM's drop is large enough to count as a mild generalisation gap.

02The problem

Ships must be inspected for coating breakdown and corrosion. Dry-docking is expensive and diver surveys are slow and risky, so an ROV with a camera is attractive. The ROV needs software that can say, for each camera frame, whether it shows corrosion and where.

Four things make this hard:

  • Imbalance. On many photos corrosion covers only a few percent of the pixels.
  • Water. Seawater absorbs red light, scatters light into haze and adds floating particles, which hides the rust colour the models rely on.
  • Near-duplicate frames. Consecutive video frames look almost identical. Splitting them at random puts copies of test images into training and inflates the scores.
  • Missed defects cost more than false alarms. A false alarm means a second look; a missed defect can mean structural damage later.

The pipeline has an offline part (prepare data, train, evaluate) and an online part (the simulated ROV runs the trained U-Net on every frame and logs the results).

Why the split groups scenes

Adjacent LoRes frames correlate at r = 0.85–0.89, so they are near-copies. Frames are grouped into scenes of five consecutive frame numbers (for example colorImage1005–1009), giving 21 scene groups. Each group goes to exactly one split, and HiRes and LoRes groups are split separately so every split contains both cameras. The ratio is 60/20/20; a 70/15/15 split would leave one scene group per camera in validation and test.

The tile task

Every MaVeCoDD photo contains some corrosion, so "does this image have a defect?" is a meaningless question: a model that always answers yes scores 100%. Instead each image is rescaled so its long side is 1024 px and cut into 256 px tiles. A tile is defect if at least 5% of its pixels are corrosion and sound if at most 0.5%. Tiles in between are skipped, because a sliver of rust at the edge should not decide the label. Both models are scored on exactly the same tiles.

05The two models

Model A · handcrafted features + SVM

Classifies each 256 px tile as defect or sound from 47 numbers:

  • 15 colour features: CIE-Lab statistics of the tile measured relative to the whole photo, which cancels each vessel's paint colour.
  • 28 texture features: uniform LBP histograms at radius 1 and 3.
  • 4 gradient features: how rough or flaky the surface is.

These feed a standardiser and an RBF-SVM with balanced class weights. The setting C = 0.03, no PCA, was picked by scene-grouped 5-fold cross-validation using the one-standard-error rule (the simplest model within one standard error of the best). Checkpoint: 0.13 MB; about 35 ms per tile on CPU.

Model B · U-Net

Predicts a corrosion probability for every pixel.

  • Four-level residual U-Net, 16 to 256 channels, 2.05 M parameters, plus a small auxiliary head that predicts whether a crop contains rust.
  • Images are resized to a 512 px long side. Training uses three random 256 px crops per image per epoch with zoom (0.8–1.6×), flips, 90° rotations, colour jitter and simulated turbidity (30% of crops).
  • Loss: BCE + 1.2 × soft Dice + 0.5 × auxiliary BCE. AdamW (lr 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4), cosine schedule, batch 8.
  • Early stopping on validation loss (patience 8) stopped at epoch 34 and kept epoch 26. Training took 35 minutes on a laptop CPU.
  • Calibrated on validation: a pixel is corrosion above probability 0.5, and a frame or tile raises an alarm when at least 8% of it is flagged.

Why the baseline does not use HOG. The first baseline used 8,154 HOG and colour-histogram features. On scenes it had not seen it reached a cross-validated balanced accuracy of only 0.78 ± 0.17 while scoring 0.97 on its own training scenes, a clear sign of overfitting. The compact 47-feature set reached 0.93 ± 0.07. The comparison used training and validation scenes only; the test set played no part in the choice.

06How results are measured

  • Dice = 2 × overlap / (predicted area + true area). 1.0 is a perfect mask. Corrosion edges are fuzzy, so even two human annotators rarely agree above about 0.8.
  • Balanced accuracy averages the hit rate on defect tiles and on sound tiles, so the large number of defect tiles cannot inflate it.
  • Recall is the share of real defects that were found; precision is the share of alarms that were real.
  • Overfitting check. Each score is computed on train, validation and test. Gap = train score − mean of validation and test. Below 0.05 is no meaningful overfitting, 0.05–0.10 is mild, above 0.10 is overfitting.
  • Confidence intervals. Test scores get a 95% interval from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of whole test images (tiles from one image are correlated, so resampling tiles would understate the uncertainty).
  • No test-set tuning. Every choice (features, hyperparameters, early stopping, thresholds) used training and validation data only.

U-Net segmentation on test images

Mean Dice 0.655, IoU 0.510, pixel recall 86%, pixel precision 57%. The U-Net misses little corrosion but over-segments: most false alarms come from deck railings and sky at the top of dry-dock photos and from weathered coating that the annotators did not mark.

The biggest drop happens at the first level, when red absorption removes rust's main colour cue. As turbidity rises the U-Net's recall goes up while Dice goes down: it over-segments hazy regions. The colour-based SVM degrades faster.

Cross-domain test

Run without retraining on 40 Underwater Crack test images, the corrosion U-Net reaches Dice 0.066 and IoU 0.038. That is expected: thin dark cracks on submerged concrete look nothing like rust on a dry-docked hull. Transfer would need labelled underwater data.

08ROV simulation

The simulator flies a virtual ROV over a ship's side and runs the U-Net on every camera frame.

  • Hull. 50 m × 10 m, ten named zones (bow to stern, keel to waterline), coverage tracked on a 0.5 m grid. Five corrosion areas are registered at fixed positions.
  • Path. A lawnmower (serpentine) path with 2.5 m and 1.2 m steps, 160 waypoints. The ROV has thruster kinematics and noisy acoustic positioning.
  • Camera. 75° field of view at 1.5 m standoff, about a 2.3 m × 1.7 m footprint. Frames are 640×480 windows cut from held-out test photos: corroded windows (≥ 10% rust) when the footprint covers a registered corrosion area, clean windows (≤ 1%) elsewhere. Windows showing sky or deck railings are excluded, because an ROV facing the hull would never see them.
  • Optics. Each frame is degraded with a Jaffe–McGlamery-style model: red absorption, backscatter haze, blur and marine snow.
  • Sensors. 19-beam LiDAR, depth and pressure, a collision monitor (warning below 0.8 m, critical below 0.5 m), an IMU and water-quality readings.
  • Scoring. A frame's ground truth is "defect" when at least 5% of it is corrosion. Every frame's alarm is compared with that ground truth.

09Telemetry and reports

Each simulation step is one record in a strictly typed Apache Arrow schema with 28 fields, written to Parquet with Snappy compression. Field groups:

  • Identity: inspection_id, step_id, timestamp
  • Pose: true and measured x, y, z and heading
  • Location: zone_id
  • Frame: camera_image_id, dataset_source, ground_truth_defect
  • Prediction: predicted_defect_class, confidence_score, defect_detected, normalised bounding box, optional mask reference
  • Environment and status: visibility, turbidity, inspection status, coverage, battery

The Inspection reports page reads these logs, scores them against ground truth, filters them, and exports CSV or a PDF survey report (ReportLab). Every detection is reported as a suspected defect that requires further inspection.

11How to run it

Requires Python 3.11 or newer. Everything runs on a CPU; a GPU is used automatically if present.

git clone https://github.com/DunD514/FOML_proj.git
cd FOML_proj
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate            # macOS / Linux: source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt

python scripts/download_datasets.py           # both datasets, SHA-256 checked
python scripts/prepare_dataset.py             # scene-grouped split manifests
python scripts/train_model.py --dataset mavecodd     # SVM ~1 min, U-Net ~35 min on CPU
python scripts/evaluate_model.py --dataset mavecodd  # train/val/test metrics, a few minutes
python scripts/run_simulation.py --steps 400 --turbidity 0.2
python -m pytest tests/ -q
streamlit run app/streamlit_app.py            # http://localhost:8501

Useful variants: --only baseline or --only unet retrains one model; --dataset sample --epochs 2 runs a quick smoke test on the synthetic plates without any download.

The trained U-Net file (*.pth) is excluded from git, so a fresh clone must run the training step before the dashboard can show predictions.

14Testing

28 pytest tests run in about 10 seconds:

  • Data (8): mask parsing, bounding boxes, degradation, leakage-free splits, scene grouping, stratification, tile labels.
  • Models (7): feature extraction, an end-to-end SVM sanity check, U-Net shapes and gradients, Dice/IoU maths, full-resolution inference, overfitting-verdict thresholds.
  • Storage (3): schema, Parquet round trip, empty logs.
  • Simulation (10): hull coverage, kinematics, camera footprint, path, LiDAR, pressure, collision, HUD, ground truth in camera frames, frame scoring.

Beyond the unit tests, every dashboard page was driven through Streamlit's test harness (navigation, all image sources and split filters, degradation, 150 simulation steps, reset), and all pages were screenshotted in light and dark mode with an automated text-contrast check.

16Limitations and open items

  • Small test set. 16 images and 21 clean tiles, so test confidence intervals are wide.
  • U-Net false alarms on railings, sky and weathered coating. More varied clean examples, or masking non-hull regions, would help.
  • Dry-dock, not underwater. MaVeCoDD photos are taken in air; underwater conditions are simulated, and the model does not transfer to underwater cracks.
  • Surface only. A camera cannot measure plate thinning or internal fatigue; that needs ultrasonic testing. Results are suspected defects for a surveyor to confirm.
  • Unverified report claims. The academic report's RQ4 (defects located within 0.15 m) and RQ5 (above 98% coverage with a re-inspection route) are not measured by any code yet.
  • Old mission logs. Three logs from 17 October were made by the old simulation and score poorly against ground truth.
